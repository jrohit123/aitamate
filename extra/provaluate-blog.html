Chapter 01Resolving the Job Description

Every hiring process starts with a job description - and most job descriptions are a mess. They're written by committee, copy-pasted from last year's version, stuffed with buzzwords, vague about what's actually required, and inconsistent in structure. If you feed a bad JD into a bad screening system, you get bad shortlists. Garbage in, garbage out.

ProValuate takes a different approach. Before anything else happens - before a single resume is uploaded - the platform reads and resolves the job description into a structured, queryable set of attributes. Think of it as translating a free-form document into a precise data model that the rest of the system can actually work with.

What does "resolving" actually mean?

When you upload a JD, our AI parses it and extracts the following dimensions automatically:

  • TTechnical skills - specific tools, languages, platforms, and frameworks that the role requires (e.g. Python, SQL, Salesforce, AutoCAD)
  • FFunctional skills - the actual job duties and domain capabilities required (e.g. Data Pipeline Development, Financial Modelling, Patient Assessment)
  • SSoft skills - interpersonal and behavioural competencies (e.g. Problem-solving mindset, Scientific thinking, Communication)
  • EEducation - degree level, field of study, and whether it's mandatory or preferred
  • XExperience - years of experience, whether it's mandatory or a range, and any specifics like "in a startup environment"
  • 📍Location - city, state, country, or remote status

The resolved JD is then stored and linked to every resume analysis and interview that runs against it. This is crucial because it means the AI isn't re-reading and re-interpreting the JD every single time - it works from a consistent, pre-processed understanding of the role. That's what makes ProValuate's evaluations consistent across hundreds of candidates, rather than drifting based on how the model is feeling that day.

✦
Why this matters for accuracy

A traditional ATS matches the words in a resume to the words in a JD. ProValuate matches the meaning behind a resume to the structured requirements extracted from a JD. The difference in result quality is significant.

Chapter 02Building Your Scoring Criteria

Once the JD is resolved, you need to decide how you want to evaluate candidates against it. This is where ProValuate's custom criteria system comes in - and it's one of the most underappreciated and yet the most powerful features on the platform.

The idea is simple: not all aspects of a role matter equally. For a junior data engineer role, technical skills might be worth 30% of the evaluation while stability might only be worth 10%. For a senior sales manager position, soft skills and communication might outweigh raw technical ability. ProValuate lets you encode exactly that logic into a scoring grid.

How the criteria grid works

You define a set of parameters - the dimensions you want to evaluate candidates on - and assign each one a percentage weighting. The weightings must sum to 100%. For each parameter, you also write a short instruction that tells the AI how to assess it when it reads a resume.

Chapter 03Deep Resume Analysis - Beyond Keyword Matching

With a resolved JD and a scoring criteria in place, you're now ready to evaluate resumes. And this is where most people genuinely start to see something different about ProValuate compared to anything they've used before.

Upload one resume or a hundred - the system processes them all the same way. For each CV, the AI performs a thorough read of the document, extracting structured information about the candidate (skills, employment history, education, projects) and then scoring them against your criteria grid with actual evidence.

What you get for every candidate

  • 📊Per-parameter scores on a 1–10 scale, each with a cited evidence excerpt from the CV itself - not just a number, but a reason
  • 🎯A weighted final score calculated from your criteria grid - so a candidate's overall match reflects the priorities you set, not some generic formula
  • 📝A detailed written assessment covering key strengths, notable gaps, experience relevance, employment history continuity, and an overall fit summary
  • 💡A recommendation - one of three clearly defined outcomes: To be interviewed, Review further, or Candidature rejected - plus a rationale and suggested follow-up questions

Consistency by design

One of the quiet but critical things ProValuate does is enforce scoring consistency. The same resume evaluated against the same JD and criteria will always produce the same score. This sounds obvious, but it's actually a hard problem with language models - they're probabilistic by nature and can produce different outputs for the same input. ProValuate uses content hashing and caching to ensure that doesn't happen here.

✓
No hallucination on scores

Scores are anchored to evidence strength: how specific, how detailed, and how technically relevant the matching content in the CV is. The AI is instructed never to give a high score without citing the actual text that justifies it.

The final scorecard is exportable as an Excel report with one row per candidate and separate columns for each parameter - making it straightforward to sort, filter, and present shortlists to stakeholders without any additional work.

Chapter 04Configuring the Interview - Your Parameters, Your Rules

Before a candidate sets foot in an interview (even a virtual, AI-conducted one), someone has to design what that interview actually tests. In traditional hiring this happens informally - an interviewer prepares a few questions, improvises the rest, and the whole thing is shaped more by mood than methodology. ProValuate makes interview design explicit, structured, and reusable.

Choosing your interview mode

The first decision is which mode you want to run. ProValuate offers two fundamentally different approaches:

🤖 AI Dynamic Mode 📝 Structured Pre-Defined Mode

In AI Dynamic Mode, you set the parameters and the system generates every question on the fly - adapting in real time to what the candidate is saying. You don't write a single question yourself. In Structured Mode, your HR team writes the exact questions that every candidate will receive - giving you full control and perfect consistency across all evaluations.

Setting up parameters for AI mode

For an AI interview, you define a set of assessment parameters - very similar in concept to the scoring criteria, but specifically designed for verbal evaluation. Each parameter comes with several configurable fields:

  1. Parameter Name & Description
    Give it a clear name (e.g. "Feature Engineering & ML Support") and a bulleted description of what it covers. This description is what the AI uses to frame its questions.
  2. Weight & Question Count
    Assign a percentage weighting and define the minimum and maximum number of questions the AI should ask for this parameter. A heavier parameter gets more airtime.
  3. Difficulty Level & Time Limit
    Set the question difficulty - Easy, Regular, or Hard - and a per-question time limit in minutes. The AI calibrates question complexity accordingly.
  4. Written Answer Toggle
    For parameters where you want a candidate to demonstrate knowledge in writing (e.g. showing a formula, a code snippet, or a structured framework), you can enable a written answer box that appears alongside the spoken response.
  5. Scoring Criteria per Parameter
    Define your own scoring rubric - what does an Excellent (9–10) response look like? What about Average (5–6)? The AI uses these to calibrate its post-interview scoring.

Once configured, the parameter setup is saved against the role name and can be reused for every subsequent interview for that position. You build it once; it runs forever.

⚡
Generate parameters automatically from the JD

Don't want to build parameters from scratch? Hit "Generate AI Parameters" and the system will read your resolved JD and suggest a full parameter set appropriate for the role - complete with descriptions, weights, and difficulty levels. You can then tweak as needed.

Chapter 05Conversational AI Interviews - Context That Actually Follows You

This is the feature most people want to understand before they commit to ProValuate - and it's the one that's hardest to describe without actually experiencing it. So let's be very precise about what's happening.

A typical AI interview tool asks a question, receives an answer, moves to the next pre-written question. It doesn't matter what the candidate said - the next question is already decided. That's not a conversation. That's a digital form with a microphone attached.

ProValuate's interview is different because it maintains the full context of everything the candidate has said before generating the next question. Every response the candidate gives is transcribed, processed, and fed back into the AI's context window before it decides where to take the conversation next.

What this looks like in practice

If a candidate answers a question about data preprocessing by casually mentioning that they've used IQR-based outlier detection in production, the AI will pick up on that - and might follow up with a more specific probe about how they handle outliers in different distribution shapes, or how they ensure the thresholds computed on training data are correctly applied to new batches. It's not guessing or hallucinating. It's reading what was actually said and responding to it.

Full speech analysis on every answer

Beyond the content of what candidates say, ProValuate also analyses how they say it. Every spoken answer is processed for:

  • 🎙Speaking pace - words per minute, compared against a comfortable conversational range
  • 💬Filler word density - count and rate of filler words like "um", "like", "basically", "you know" - a reliable proxy for uncertainty
  • 📊Voice stress score - derived from jitter and shimmer analysis of the audio, indicating perceived anxiety levels
  • 🔊Voice confidence & modulation - pitch variation, intensity, and engagement score
  • ⏸Pause analysis - categorised as breathing, thoughtful, or awkward pauses, with per-pause duration timing

All of this is compiled into a detailed speech feedback report per answer, and a final overall speech analysis report for the full interview. Candidates also receive a personalised action plan - a structured set of improvement recommendations based on their specific speech patterns and content weaknesses.

"The interview doesn't just evaluate what you know. It understands how confidently you know it - and the difference between those two things turns out to be enormously important in real hiring decisions."

- ProValuate Interview Design Team

Personalised opening questions from the CV

Optionally, you can enable personalised questions - where the AI reads the candidate's own resume and crafts a set of custom opening questions that reference their specific background before moving into the standard parameter-based assessment. This creates a more natural, less robotic feel from the very first exchange and gives candidates the sense that someone has actually read their CV.

Chapter 06Interview Types & Modes - Choosing the Right Assessment for the Role

Different roles demand different kinds of evaluation. A software engineer needs to be tested on what they can build. A customer success manager needs to demonstrate how they handle conflict. A team lead needs to show both. ProValuate provides three interview types to map to these different needs, plus two distinct modes that control how the interview is delivered.

The three interview types

Functional Behavioral Mixed

Functional interviews focus on role-specific hard skills and domain knowledge - the things the person will actually be doing day-to-day. For a data scientist, that means statistical methods, ML pipelines, and production deployment. For a nurse, it means clinical procedures, patient assessment, and regulatory compliance. The AI knows the difference because the parameters you configure describe the domain explicitly.

Behavioral interviews shift the focus to soft skills, interpersonal effectiveness, and professional attributes - assessed through real examples from the candidate's past. The AI prompts for STAR-method responses (Situation, Task, Action, Result) and evaluates the quality and clarity of the examples given. Parameters in a behavioral interview look like "Cross-Functional Collaboration," "Resilience Under Pressure," or "Stakeholder Communication."

Mixed interviews do exactly what you'd expect - they blend both functional and behavioral evaluation in whatever ratio makes sense for the role. A 50/50 split is the default, but you can skew it toward whichever dimension is more critical by adjusting parameter weights accordingly.

The two delivery modes

Independently of type, every interview is delivered in one of two modes:

In AI Dynamic Mode, the AI generates every question live, based on the parameters you've configured and the real-time context of the conversation. No two interviews are identical - each one is shaped by what the candidate says. This is the mode most employers use for mid-to-senior roles where nuance matters.

In Structured Mode, your HR team writes the exact questions upfront using the HR Interview Creator. Every candidate who goes through this role receives precisely the same questions in the same order. This is ideal for high-volume hiring, regulated industries, or situations where legal defensibility of the evaluation process is important. Scores are still AI-generated and evidence-based - but the question set is human-controlled.

The ProValuate DifferenceTraditional ATS vs. ChatGPT vs. ProValuate

Before we wrap up, it's worth addressing the question every recruiter eventually asks: "We already use an ATS. We've also tried pasting CVs into ChatGPT. Why do we need ProValuate?" It's a fair question - and the answer isn't just marketing. The three approaches are fundamentally different in what they can and cannot do, and understanding those differences changes how you think about the cost of getting it wrong.

The Traditional ATS: Fast, Scalable, and Shallow

Applicant Tracking Systems were built to solve a volume problem, not a quality problem. When your company receives five hundred applications for a single role, you need a way to stop looking at most of them quickly. ATS tools do this by scanning resumes for keyword matches against the job description - if the JD says "Python" and the resume says "Python," that's a match. If it doesn't, it isn't.

The problem is that keyword matching has almost nothing to do with whether someone can actually do the job. A candidate who spent three years building production ML pipelines in Python but wrote "machine learning engineering" on their CV instead of "Python developer" might score lower than a bootcamp graduate who keyword-stuffed their resume correctly. The ATS doesn't know the difference. It was never designed to.

⚠️
The ATS blind spot

Studies consistently show that over 75% of qualified candidates are screened out by ATS systems before a human ever sees their resume - not because they're unqualified, but because their resume wasn't optimised for keyword matching. The system filters for resume writers, not for talent.

There's also no nuance in an ATS about how much a skill matters. "Must have Python" and "Nice to have SQL" carry equal weight in a keyword scan. There's no concept of weighting, no understanding of context, and no ability to distinguish between someone who used a tool once in a university project versus someone who's been deploying it in production for five years.

ChatGPT / Generic LLMs: Contextual, But Dangerously Inconsistent

The natural response to ATS limitations has been to reach for a large language model. Paste the job description and a CV into ChatGPT, ask it to assess the candidate, and you get back something that feels remarkably intelligent - nuanced observations, identification of genuine strengths and gaps, a recommendation. For the first CV you process this way, it feels like a revelation.

Then you process the second one. And the tenth. And you start to notice something uncomfortable: the same CV, processed twice, gives you slightly different results. The model's output is probabilistic - it's not anchored to a fixed evaluation framework, so it drifts. A CV processed on Monday might score a 7. The same CV on Friday, with slightly different phrasing in the prompt, might score a 5. You have no way of knowing which one is right, or whether either is.

⚠️
The hallucination and consistency problem

Generic LLMs have no memory of your hiring standards, no enforced evaluation framework, and no consistency mechanism. Every prompt is stateless. The AI might praise a candidate for a skill that isn't in the JD, or miss a critical gap because it wasn't specifically instructed to look for it. At scale, this becomes a serious liability.

The deeper issue is that ChatGPT has no understanding of your hiring priorities. It doesn't know that for this particular role, stability matters more than raw technical skills. It doesn't know that you've decided education should be weighted at 15% while experience counts for 25%. It applies a generic, averaged sense of "good candidate" that reflects its training data - not your hiring philosophy. And when you're making decisions that affect people's careers and your company's future, "generic and averaged" is not a defensible standard.

ProValuate: Structured, Consistent, and Built for Decisions

ProValuate was designed with the explicit goal of keeping what's good about both approaches while eliminating what makes them dangerous at scale. It uses the contextual understanding of a large language model, but constrains it within a structured framework that you control - so the intelligence is consistently applied rather than randomly expressed.

The resolved JD means the AI is always working from the same structured understanding of the role, not re-reading and re-interpreting a raw text document each time. The criteria grid means every candidate is evaluated against the same weighted parameters that you defined - not a generic sense of what makes a good hire. The content hashing and caching means the same CV against the same criteria always produces the same score, full stop.

And none of this is a black box. Every score comes with a cited evidence excerpt from the actual CV. Every recommendation comes with a written rationale. Every interview answer is scored against the rubric you wrote. You can audit any decision, defend any shortlist, and explain to any candidate exactly why they received the evaluation they did.

Final ThoughtsWhy This Actually Changes Hiring

Hiring has been running on gut feel dressed up in process for decades. Structured interviews help but are inconsistently applied. ATS tools filter resumes but filter the wrong ones. Reference checks verify almost nothing. And somewhere in this fog, people make decisions that affect entire careers based on a 45-minute conversation and a one-page document.

ProValuate doesn't eliminate human judgment - it shouldn't, and it doesn't try to. What it does is give that human judgment something real to work with. A structured scorecard built from criteria you designed. An AI interview transcript that surfaces exactly what the candidate knows and how confidently they know it. A speech analysis that adds a dimension of assessment that humans can't reliably do themselves at scale.

The goal was never to replace recruiters. It was to make every recruiter work as if they had a team of expert analysts behind them - one that's available at 3am, never gets tired, never gets biased by a firm handshake, and never misses a candidate because their resume used different vocabulary to describe the same skill.

That's what ProValuate is built to do. And based on everything described in this post - from JD resolution to real-time interview intelligence to inbox plugins - we think it's getting pretty close.
