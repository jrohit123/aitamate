5-Point Automation Audit Checklist

Find Your Hidden Automation Opportunities

A practical worksheet to identify where delays, manual work, and inefficiency cost your business the most • aitamate Solutions

How to Use This Checklist

This checklist is designed to be completed as a team exercise. Set aside 60-90 minutes in a workshop setting. Go through each of the 5 points with your operations, HR, or finance team. Be honest about where friction exists.

Pro tip: Don't overthink this. Your gut instinct about where problems exist is usually right. The goal is to surface opportunities, not solve them (yet).

What you'll get out of this:

  • A clear list of 5-10 processes that could be automated
  • Quantified understanding of how much time/money each opportunity represents
  • A prioritized view of which to tackle first (usually not what you think)
  • Data to present to leadership and justify investment
1

Where Is Your Manual Labor Bleeding Away?

The question: Who on your team spends the most time on work that could be done by a machine or a simple script?

Look for:

  • Data entry into multiple systems
  • Copy-pasting information between tools
  • Formatting reports or documents from raw data
  • Manually screening, sorting, or filtering information
  • Repetitive email or notification tasks

Your Worksheet:

Person/Role: _________________________________
Task: _____________________________________
Hours per week: ___ Hours [estimate]
Cost (hours × hourly rate): ₹ ____________

What's the impact if we do nothing? _____________________

Record 3-5 examples of manual labor you observed.

2

Which Tasks Repeat 5+ Times Per Week?

The question: What workflows run over and over? Every day or multiple times daily?

Why this matters: Repetitive tasks are automation gold. They're predictable, easily testable, and deliver consistent ROI.

Look for:

  • Status check-ins or status updates
  • Data validation or quality checks
  • Schedule coordination or calendar management
  • Invoice processing or payment approvals
  • Daily/weekly report generation
  • Customer or employee communication templated messages

Your Worksheet:

Recurring Task: __________________________
Frequency: ___ times per week [or per day]
Time per cycle: ___ minutes
Total time/week: ___ hours

Who owns this task? ___________________________

What would change if it was instant/automated? ____________

Record your top 5-8 repetitive tasks.

3

How Much Decision-Data Are You Manually Compiling?

The question: Where does someone have to hunt through multiple systems, reports, or files to make a decision?

Why this matters: Pulling data from 10 different sources before making a decision is automation looking for a home. Automating data *visibility* is often more valuable than automating the task itself.

Look for:

  • Weekly/monthly reporting cycles that take hours
  • Status meetings where people read out metrics manually
  • Decision backlogs waiting for data compilation
  • Critical decisions delayed because "we need to check three systems"
  • Multiple tools tracking the same information (data duplicated across systems)

Your Worksheet:

Decision Point: ___________________________
Data sources involved: ___ systems [list them]
Time to compile: ___ hours monthly
Who needs this data? ____________________

How would better visibility change this decision? ___________

Record 3-4 decision-data compilation tasks.

4

Where Is Team Productivity Lost to Admin Work?

The question: Where do your most valuable people spend time on admin instead of doing what they were hired to do?

Why this matters: This is where automation has the highest ROI. One ops manager saving 10 hours/week to focus on strategy = millions in value. This is the conversation that justifies investment.

Look for:

  • Managers spending time on scheduling, coordination, or logistics instead of coaching
  • Sales people doing data entry instead of selling
  • HR teams managing onboarding checklists instead of culture building
  • Finance teams reconciling spreadsheets instead of strategic planning
  • Leaders doing manual tasks that junior staff could do (if they had time)

Your Worksheet:

High-value role: ____________________________
Admin task stealing their time: ________________
Hours per week on this task: ___ hours
Their annual salary: ₹ ___ Lakhs

What would this person focus on if they had those hours back? ____

Record 3-5 instances where top talent is stuck in admin mode.

5

What Would You Build If You Had 10 Extra Hours Every Week?

The question: If your team had 10 hours of freed-up capacity per week, what would change?

Why this matters: This question surfaces the true business value. It's not about automation for its own sake—it's about what becomes possible once you're not drowning in repetitive work.

Examples:

  • "We'd finally have time to improve our hiring process and reduce bad hires"
  • "We could build that customer portal we've been planning for 18 months"
  • "We could actually analyze our data instead of just collecting it"
  • "We could mentor junior staff instead of firefighting every day"
  • "We could finally scale to the next customer segment"

Your Worksheet:

If we automated [task from Points 1-4],
we would have the capacity to:
1. ________________________________________
2. ________________________________________
3. ________________________________________

Business impact of doing this: ₹ ____ or ____ new customers or ____ in time savings

Brain-dump all the ideas. The best automation opportunities are the ones that unblock something bigger.

Scoring & Prioritization Matrix

Now that you've identified opportunities, it's time to prioritize. Use this matrix to score each opportunity on two dimensions: Impact (how much it would save/improve) and Feasibility (how easy/realistic it is to automate).

Target: Usually, the best first automation is HIGH IMPACT + HIGH FEASIBILITY (quick win). Then tackle HIGH IMPACT + MEDIUM FEASIBILITY (bigger plays). Avoid LOW IMPACT even if feasible (not worth your energy).

Scoring Guide

Impact (1-5)

  • 5 = Eliminates ₹50L+ annual cost or enables major new revenue
  • 4 = Saves ₹20-50L annually or frees up 10+ person-hours/week
  • 3 = Saves ₹5-20L or frees up 5-10 person-hours/week
  • 2 = Saves ₹1-5L or frees up 2-5 person-hours/week
  • 1 = Saves <₹1L or saves <2 hours/week

Feasibility (1-5)

  • 5 = Can be done with existing tools, no coding, <1 week
  • 4 = Straightforward API integration or RPA, 2-4 weeks
  • 3 = Doable but requires custom logic or data work, 4-8 weeks
  • 2 = Complex, requires multiple tool changes or data migration, 8-12 weeks
  • 1 = Very complex, custom build required, 3+ months

What's Next?

Congratulations. You've just done 40% of a professional process audit. You've identified problems and quantified their impact.

Here's what you still need:

  • Prioritization based on dependencies — These opportunities don't exist in isolation. Automating process B might unlock process A. A professional audit sequences them optimally.
  • Realistic ROI and timeline — Your estimates are usually conservative. A pro can tell you actual costs, timelines, and risk factors.
  • Tool and technology selection — Which tools to use? Build vs. buy vs. integrate? That decision alone can make or break ROI.
  • Implementation support — Identifying opportunities is one thing. Executing them successfully is another. Most automation projects fail not on the tools, but on change management.

The opportunities are real. The question is: how do you want to execute them?
